# Pattern Transfer — Proven Companies → Unbuilt Verticals

**Date:** September 7, 2026
**Third in the series.** First memo = recent deals + nine theses. Second = eleven new ideas + how to
differentiate. This one = the mechanical version: take patterns that have *already* attracted
capital, and enumerate the verticals where the same pattern is unbuilt.

**How to read this.** Each pattern gives you the anchor companies (with what they raised), the
transferable mechanism, a **fit test** for choosing a vertical, then two lists: **taken** (do not
walk in blind) and **open**. The fit test matters more than the list — it's what lets you generate
your own verticals instead of picking from mine.

---

## The universal scorecard

Score any vertical 0–2 on each. **10+ is a company; under 7 is a side project.**

1. **Forcing function** — is there a mandatory artifact (a note, a claim, a filing, an inspection
   report) someone is legally required to produce?
2. **Money moves on it** — does the artifact directly trigger payment, reimbursement, or liability?
3. **Labor is the top cost line** — if the answer is software licenses, there's no budget to convert.
4. **Fragmented buyers, concentrated channel** — thousands of small buyers is fine *only* if there's
   a franchise, association, GPO, EHR/PMS vendor, or lender you can ride.
5. **Incumbent software is 15+ years old** — check for a Windows client, an on-prem server, or a
   company acquired by private equity twice.
6. **Data exhaust accrues to you** — after 1,000 customers, do you know something nobody can buy?
7. **First ten customers reachable without an enterprise sales team** — can a founder close one on a
   phone call in week three?

## Pattern 1 — Ambient capture → structured record

**Anchors:** Abridge (~$5.3B valuation, $100M+ ARR, 250+ health systems), Ambience ($243M),
Nabla ($24M), Voize ($50M, nurses), Corti. Vet analogues already funded: HappyDoc, Vetnio/VetsGPT.

**Mechanism:** a human is talking anyway; the documentation is legally required, hated, and
determines payment. Record it, structure it, file it.

**Fit test:** spoken encounter + mandatory artifact + payment or liability attached to the artifact.

**Taken:** physician ambient (over), nursing (Voize), veterinary SOAP notes (HappyDoc, Vetnio,
Digitail $23M), dental imaging/workflow (VideaHealth $40M, Overjet, Pearl).

**Open verticals:**
- **EMS / ambulance run reports** — crews document in a moving vehicle, the report drives a claim
  that gets denied at brutal rates, and the software is genuinely ancient. Best-in-class fit.
- **Long-term care MDS assessments** — the assessment literally sets the reimbursement rate (PDPM);
  errors are audited and clawed back. Money moves on the artifact more directly than almost anywhere.
- **ABA and behavioral therapy session notes** — payers require session-level documentation for
  every unit billed; technicians write them after hours.
- **Home health OASIS assessments** — same forcing function as MDS, different form.
- **School special education / IEP evaluations** — federally mandated, deadline-driven, and
  districts are sued over lateness.
- **Child welfare and social work case notes** — court-facing, chronically backlogged.
- **Correctional health encounters** — mandated documentation, consent-decree scrutiny, no vendors.
- **Physical therapy, chiropractic, audiology** — per-visit notes tied to per-visit billing.
- **Clinical research monitoring visits** — monitors write trip reports; sponsors need them for
  inspection readiness.
- **Field insurance adjusting / FNOL inspections** — the adjuster's narrative *is* the payment.
- **Utility and telecom field inspections; mining and rail safety walks** — regulator-facing reports.
- **Agronomist field visits** — ties into YC's low-pesticide-agriculture RFS.
- **Veterinary specialty and equine** — the generalist vet scribes don't cover it.

## Pattern 2 — Autonomous back office for a regulated payment cycle

**Anchors:** Arintra ($25M, autonomous coding), Overdrive Health (billing), ClaimGlide (prior auth),
Assort Health ($120M at $1.2B, patient access), Cohere Health, Pearl Health ($110M).

**Mechanism:** a claim/authorization moves through a rules-heavy pipeline staffed by humans reading
documents. Automate it and get paid on the recovery, not the seat.

**Fit test:** an adversarial payer + a denial rate anyone can quote you + contingency pricing is
already normal in the industry.

**Taken:** physician/hospital coding, prior auth, patient access, Medicare Advantage admin.

**Open verticals:**
- **EMS/ambulance billing** — highest denial rates in healthcare, smallest tech budgets, and the
  billing companies are acquirable.
- **DME and home infusion** — documentation requirements are baroque, audits are constant.
- **Workers' compensation medical billing** — 50 state fee schedules, a rules engine nobody has
  rebuilt since the 90s.
- **Behavioral health and ABA authorizations** — unit-based auth renewals every few weeks.
- **Dental claims and attachments** — VideaHealth is going here from imaging; the pure claims side
  is open.
- **Veterinary insurance claims** — pet insurance is growing fast with a manual claims back office.
- **Clinical lab and pathology billing** — high volume, low dollar, brutal denials.
- **Medicaid school-based claiming** — districts leave enormous money unclaimed; almost nobody knows
  this market exists.
- **Non-emergency medical transport, hospice, dialysis** — same shape, each unserved.
- Outside health, identical mechanics: **freight invoice audit**, **construction pay applications
  and lien waivers**, **utility bill audit**, **R&D tax credit substantiation**, **film/TV production
  tax credits**, **customs classification and duty drawback**.

## Pattern 3 — AI-native services roll-up (own the P&L; AI is the margin)

**Anchors:** Allia Health (mental health CIG, $50M+ annualized contracts, 8,000+ clinicians),
Adaptive Innovations ($60M, one of Texas's largest home health providers, ~80% documentation-time
cut), and outside healthcare the strategy is now a category: General Catalyst's Creation Fund
($800M → **$1.5B**), Thrive's **$1B** vehicle, **$3B+** deployed across the majors. Crete/Current
(Thrive-backed accounting) passed **$300M revenue across 30+ firms**; Eudia raised **$105M** (legal);
Shield Technology Partners is rolling up MSPs. GC claims some portfolio companies double EBITDA
margins within 12 months.

**Mechanism:** buy fragmented, licensed, labor-heavy businesses at 3–5x EBITDA, install one AI-native
system of record, expand margin, re-rate the multiple.

**Fit test:** licensed/credentialed + labor is >50% of cost + recurring revenue + owner retiring +
documentation-bound + no dominant software vendor.

**Taken:** accounting, law, MSPs, property management, home health, mental health.

**Open verticals:**
- **Outpatient PT, occupational and speech therapy** — huge, fragmented, insurance-reimbursed.
- **Optometry and audiology** — retail economics plus clinical licensure; PE is there but with no tech.
- **IDD / HCBS provider agencies** — massive Medicaid-funded labor businesses, worst software in
  healthcare, and states are desperate for capacity.
- **Hospice and palliative** — documentation-driven reimbursement, heavy audit exposure.
- **Wound care, infusion, sleep, fertility** — each is a specialty with a documentation tax.
- **Dental service organizations** in secondary markets.
- **Veterinary clinic groups** — consolidators paused after the 2021–22 overpay; assets are cheap now.
- **Medical billing companies themselves** — buy the RCM shops, run Pattern 2 inside them. This is
  the single most under-discussed version: you own the customer relationship *and* the workflow.
- **Clinical research sites** — see Pattern 5.
- Non-health, still open: **title agencies**, **insurance brokerages**, **permit expediting**,
  **land surveying**, **environmental/EHS consulting**, **staffing agencies**, **veterinary and
  dental labs**, **court reporting**, **medical transcription** (yes, still).

## Pattern 4 — Foundation model on one proprietary modality

**Anchors:** Blank Bio ($7.2M seed, RNA foundation models + a **PacBio** partnership — note the
shape: AI lab marries instrument maker), Atlas Discovery (patient drug response), TareBio (immune
repertoire), Superluminal ($60M with **NVIDIA** and **Eli Lilly** on the cap table).

**Mechanism:** pick a data modality with a corporate owner who wants a model and cannot build one.
Trade model expertise for exclusive data access.

**Fit test:** the modality has (a) an instrument or registry owner, (b) no public foundation model,
(c) a downstream decision worth paying for.

**Open modalities:**
- **Clinical:** spatial transcriptomics, immune repertoire, mass-spec proteomics, microbiome, EEG,
  ambulatory ECG, gait/motion capture, ultrasound video, capsule endoscopy, colposcopy,
  ophthalmic OCT, dermoscopy, audiometry, polysomnography, voice and cough biomarkers,
  fetal monitoring, ICU waveform data (the most under-modeled dataset in the hospital).
- **Adjacent:** veterinary imaging and labs, dental radiographs, forensic pathology.
- **Non-clinical, same play:** soil spectroscopy, hyperspectral satellite, seismic, industrial
  vibration, grid waveform, wastewater epidemiology, materials spectroscopy, food-safety sensor data.

**The move to copy:** Blank Bio's real innovation was the *partnership structure*. Find the PacBio of
your modality — the instrument vendor sitting on data it can't monetize — before you write a line of
model code.

## Pattern 5 — Answer engine for a licensed profession

**Anchor:** OpenEvidence (~$250M Series D at **$12B**, reportedly used by ~40% of US physicians).

**Mechanism:** a licensed professional carries personal liability for decisions, the governing corpus
is public but fragmented, and being wrong is expensive.

**Fit test:** personal license at risk + public-but-fragmented corpus + the answer changes a billable
action. (Not "would they enjoy a search engine" — would they *fear* not having one.)

**Open verticals:** nurses and NPs (different question set entirely), pharmacists (interactions,
compounding, 503B rules), veterinarians, dentists, **regulatory affairs professionals**,
**clinical research coordinators**, immigration attorneys, patent agents, actuaries, safety
engineers (OSHA), building code officials, environmental compliance, food safety (FSMA), export
control (ITAR/EAR), maritime, aviation maintenance (AD compliance), utilities (NERC CIP), insurance
underwriters, and school district compliance officers.

## Pattern 6 — Retrieval as a toll road

**Anchor:** Hubble (YC S26, medical records retrieval API — team from Grow Therapy and One Medical).

**Mechanism:** every agent in a vertical needs the same input; each is building its own scrapers.
Sell the input.

**Open verticals:** veterinary records, dental records, school/IEP records, court records, insurance
loss runs, property and title records, employment and licensure verification, equipment maintenance
logs, customs and shipping documents, utility usage histories, farm and herd records.

## Pattern 7 — Voice front desk

**Anchors:** Assort Health ($120M at $1.2B), Hyro (Define portfolio), Patientdesk.ai (dental).

**Fit test:** inbound call volume + hourly staff who quit + a booking or dispatch action at the end.

**Open verticals:** veterinary practices, imaging centers and labs, pharmacies, DME suppliers, home
care intake, dialysis scheduling; outside health: property management maintenance lines, auto
service, municipal 311, utilities, legal intake, insurance FNOL, trades dispatch, funeral homes.

## Pattern 8 — Consumer data-loop health

**Anchors:** Illume Labs (YC S26, wearables + labs + genomics), Happy Health ($75M), the whole
GLP-1/peptide consumer ecosystem (the #2 funded indication in H1 2026).

**Fit test:** a measurable biomarker + a cash-paying, anxious, underserved cohort + a repeat
purchase.

**Open verticals:** perimenopause and menopause, male hormone health, pediatric growth and puberty,
adolescent mental health, chronic pain, autoimmune flare prediction, long COVID and ME/CFS,
addiction recovery maintenance, post-partum recovery, athlete/team performance, **pet longevity**
(follows Gamgee's mRNA-for-dogs logic), and caregiver-facing dementia monitoring.

## Pattern 9 — Regulatory intelligence, per regime

**Anchor:** the shape proven inside pharma/device regulatory work, and the reason Define's thesis puts
"data foundation" under everything.

**Fit test:** a regulator publishes continuously + non-compliance is fined + companies staff a
department to track it.

**Open regimes:** EPA, OSHA, FSMA (food), EU AI Act conformity, CSRD/ESG, BSA/AML, FINRA/SEC,
MiCA/crypto, cannabis (state-by-state), building and energy codes, FCC, FAA airworthiness
directives, NERC CIP, gaming/gambling licensure, state insurance filings, ITAR/EAR export control,
and state Medicaid policy bulletins (the most valuable and least served of the lot).

## Pattern 10 — Physical AI in a dirty vertical

**Anchors:** Grip Robotics (YC S26, grippers for waste and material handling), 56 industrial
companies in S26 alone, plus YC's RFS on hardware supply chain, low-pesticide agriculture, and
industrial capability.

**Fit test:** repetitive physical task + chronic labor shortage + tolerant of 95% accuracy + an
existing outsourced-services budget you can convert.

**Open verticals:** hospital sterile processing (SPD is a national staffing crisis nobody addresses),
lab specimen handling and accessioning, pharmacy compounding, food processing and protein plants,
textile and garment, recycling and e-waste, greenhouse and vertical farming, poultry and dairy,
construction rebar and drywall, ship hull and tank inspection, dental and orthodontic lab work.

## The traps

1. **The vertical is too poor.** If a buyer can't sustain $10–25k/year, you need thousands of them
   and a channel you don't have. Check the median practice's revenue *before* the customer discovery.
2. **The pattern is taken and you didn't check.** Vet scribes, dental imaging, physician ambient,
   accounting roll-ups — all crowded. A funded incumbent isn't automatically disqualifying, but you
   need a reason beyond "we'll build it better."
3. **No forcing function.** "Saves them time" loses to "they get audited." Rank verticals by the
   consequence of *not* having the artifact.
4. **Roll-ups aren't software.** You take on debt, employees, licensure, and clinical or professional
   liability, and you're valued on services multiples until you prove the margin story. The metric
   that decides it is same-store contribution margin by clinic vintage inside 12 months.
5. **Owning nothing.** After the pilot, ask what you keep — if the model, the data and the customer
   relationship all belong to someone else, you built a feature.

## Where I'd start, given your background

Ranked for *your* unfair advantage (regulatory, safety, clinical documentation):

1. **EMS billing + run-report capture as one company** (Patterns 1 + 2). Worst denial rates, oldest
   software, contingency pricing already normal, and the billing companies are cheap to acquire.
2. **Long-term care MDS / home health OASIS** (Pattern 1). The assessment *is* the reimbursement
   rate — the tightest artifact-to-dollar link in American healthcare.
3. **Regulatory intelligence for state Medicaid policy** (Pattern 9). Every provider and MCO in a
   state needs it, the bulletins are public and chaotic, and nobody is building it.
4. **ICU waveform foundation model** (Pattern 4), if you want the research-flavored version — find
   the monitor vendor who is your PacBio.
